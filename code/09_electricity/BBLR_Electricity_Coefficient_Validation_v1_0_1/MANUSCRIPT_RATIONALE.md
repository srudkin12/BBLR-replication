# Rationale for the empirical coefficient-validation audit

The simulation and empirical sections face different observability problems. In the simulations, the derivative function is known by construction. Feasible tuning still uses outcome loss only, but derivative performance can subsequently be scored against the true derivative on independent test support and on a fixed grid. Nothing is gained by replacing that direct truth-based score with a proxy based on observed outcome contrasts.

In the electricity application, the true derivative is not observed. Reporting only outcome RMSE would therefore leave the coefficient curves largely unevaluated even though coefficient-field recovery is a central target of the paper. The coefficient-validation audit uses a restriction implied by the maintained conditional-mean model: for two temperatures `x_1 < x_2`, the conditional-mean difference equals the integral of the derivative over that interval. Held-out outcome differences are noisy realizations of this contrast. Evaluating several frozen derivative fields on the same held-out pairs therefore provides an empirical check on whether their local slopes are consistent with out-of-sample changes in the response.

The audit does not introduce a new estimator-selection rule. Every configuration is frozen at the previously selected outcome-CV value, and the contrast criterion is evaluated only afterwards on fresh cross-fitting partitions. It is consequently analogous in timing to the truth-based derivative evaluation in the Monte Carlo exercises, while necessarily weaker in interpretation because empirical derivative truth remains latent.

The manuscript should therefore describe the audit as an **empirical coefficient-field validation diagnostic** or **held-out local-contrast audit**, not as direct derivative MSE and not as proof that one fitted derivative is the truth. Its value is comparative: if one field predicts held-out local changes more accurately, remains stable under week-grouped validation, and exhibits economically coherent sign transitions, the evidence for that coefficient field is stronger than smoothness or outcome RMSE alone would imply.
